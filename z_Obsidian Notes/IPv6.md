## Definition

**IPv6 (Internet Protocol version 6)** is the newer IP version with 128-bit addressing, designed to overcome IPv4 address limits and modernize network-layer behavior.

## Key Characteristics

- 128-bit address space (extremely large scale).
- Hexadecimal, colon-separated notation (for example `2001:db8::1`).
- Simplified base header compared with IPv4 plus extension-header flexibility.

## Operational Notes

- Reduces reliance on widespread address-sharing practices used with IPv4.
- Supports stateless address autoconfiguration in many deployments.
- Often introduced gradually through dual-stack networks alongside [[IPv4]].

## Why It Matters

IPv6 enables long-term Internet growth, cleaner end-to-end addressing models, and improved scalability for globally connected devices.
